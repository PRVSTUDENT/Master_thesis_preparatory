Viscoelasticity can generate time-dependent hysteresis, stress relaxation, and delayed recovery, but it is not the correct constitutive framework for true metallic cyclic plasticity. Metallic cyclic plasticity is governed by irreversible plastic flow, yield-surface evolution, reverse-loading effects such as the Bauschinger effect, and possible ratcheting under non-zero mean load. These mechanisms are addressed by plasticity-based hardening models, not by a purely viscoelastic formulation. Therefore, in the present thesis context, viscoelasticity serves only as a conceptual contrast and not as a suitable main model for cyclic plastic response in metals.
